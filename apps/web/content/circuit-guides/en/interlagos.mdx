## Interlagos in one sentence

A short, anticlockwise lap with big elevation changes, mixing a slow, twisty middle sector with a long flat-out climb to the line.

The track is named after José Carlos Pace, the Brazilian driver who won the Grand Prix here in 1975. Almost everyone still calls it Interlagos, after the São Paulo neighbourhood it sits in.

## Key corners

- **The Senna S (turns 1 and 2).** At the end of the pit straight, a downhill left followed by a right. It's the main overtaking spot: drivers arrive with the slipstream of the whole climb.
- **Descida do Lago (turn 4).** A left at the end of the back straight, also downhill. A second chance to pass on the same lap.
- **The middle sector.** From turn 6 to turn 11, slow linked corners with little room to pass. This is where qualifying time is won.
- **Junção (turn 12).** The last slow corner before the climb to the line. A good exit decides your speed all the way up the straight: a mistake here costs you until turn 1.

## What kind of car it suits

One with good traction out of slow corners that doesn't lose top speed on the climb. With two long straight sections, a low-downforce set-up helps both to defend and to attack, but the middle sector punishes a car that won't turn.

## Strategy and weather

The weather changes fast at Interlagos. The 2016 and 2024 races were run in the rain and stopped with a red flag: on days like that, timing the switch to wet tyres matters more than any plan.

## A moment in history

2008, the final race of the year. Felipe Massa won at home and was champion for a few seconds. On the last lap, in the rain, Lewis Hamilton passed Timo Glock in the final corners, finished fifth and took the title by one point.
