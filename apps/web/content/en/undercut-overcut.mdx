The undercut means pitting before a rival to gain time on fresh tyres; the overcut means staying out longer to gain it in clean air.

## The idea

A new tyre is faster than a worn one. Stop a lap or two before the car ahead and you'll run those laps on fresh rubber while they're still on old tyres. When they pit, they may come out behind you. That's the undercut.

The overcut is the opposite: you stay out while your rival pits. It works when their new tyres take time to warm up, or when they rejoin in traffic while you push in clean air.

## When each one works

- **Undercut:** when the gap between new and used tyres is large and the tyre warms up quickly.
- **Overcut:** when new tyres are slow to reach their best, in cold conditions, or when the rival rejoins in traffic.
- **Neither:** when the gap on track is bigger than what one or two laps can make up.
