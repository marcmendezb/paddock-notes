The safety car bunches the whole field behind it after an incident; the VSC neutralises the race without a car, forcing everyone to slow down.

## The idea

When a car is stopped or there's debris on track, race control neutralises the race. Under the safety car the field closes up and the gaps disappear. Under the virtual safety car (VSC), every driver must respect a minimum time per section, so the gaps stay.

## Why it changes strategy

A pit stop costs around 20 seconds at racing speed, but much less when the field is going slowly. A well-timed safety car can hand out a "cheap" stop.

## A real example

At the 2026 Mexico City Grand Prix there was a VSC on laps 7 and 8, a safety car on lap 44 that turned into a red flag, and another between laps 59 and 62.

## How to spot it in our charts

In the [strategy](/en/analysis/2026/19/strategy) and [pace](/en/analysis/2026/19/pace) charts, shaded bands mark neutralised periods. Pace hides them by default, because those laps aren't useful for comparison.
