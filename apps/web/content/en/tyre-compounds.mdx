There are three dry compounds per Grand Prix (soft, medium and hard) and two wet ones (intermediate and full wet); softer means more grip and less life.

## The idea

The tyre supplier brings three dry compounds to each race, picked from a wider range for each circuit. The soft is the fastest and wears first; the hard is the slowest and lasts longest. On a wet track there are intermediates for light water and full wets for heavy rain.

## The two-compound rule

In a dry race every driver must use at least two different dry compounds. That's why almost everyone stops at least once. The rule doesn't apply when wet-weather tyres are used. It's a sporting regulation, so we review it at the start of every season.

## How to spot it in our charts

In the [tyre strategy](/en/analysis) each compound has its own pattern and letter (S, M, H, I, W), so you can tell them apart without relying on colour.
