A Formula 1 race makes sense with five ideas: the weekend, points, tyres, pit stops and neutralisations. This guide explains them in ten minutes.

## 1. The weekend

Friday and Saturday bring practice and qualifying; Sunday is race day. Sprint weekends add a short race on Saturday. More in [the sprint format](/en/learn/sprint).

## 2. Who races

Eleven teams with two drivers each. There are two championships: drivers' and constructors', which adds up both cars' points for each team.

## 3. How to win

The top ten score, from 25 points down to 1. Starting ahead matters because overtaking is hard, which is why [qualifying](/en/learn/qualifying) is key. More in [how points work](/en/learn/points).

## 4. Tyres

They change races more than anything else. Softs are fast but don't last; hards are the opposite. More in [tyre compounds](/en/learn/tyre-compounds) and [degradation](/en/learn/tyre-degradation).

## 5. Pit stops

Almost everyone stops at least once to change tyres. Choosing when is strategy: [undercut and overcut](/en/learn/undercut-overcut).

## 6. Flags and the safety car

After an incident, the race is neutralised with a safety car, VSC or red flag. A stop at that moment costs less time. More in [safety car and VSC](/en/learn/safety-car).

## 7. How to read the timing screen

The interval is the gap to the car ahead; the gap to leader is the gap to first place. Each lap is split into three sectors to show where time is gained.

## 8. How to follow a race on Paddock Notes

Before the race, check the [calendar](/en/races) with session times in your time zone. Afterwards, see the [result](/en/races) and, a few hours later, the [analysis](/en/analysis): strategy, pace, telemetry, sectors and lap-by-lap positions.
