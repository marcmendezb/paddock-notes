Dirty air is the turbulent air a car leaves behind; it robs the following car of downforce and makes passing in corners harder.

## The idea

A Formula 1 car is pushed onto the track by the air flowing over its wings and under its floor. When you follow another car closely, that air arrives churned up and your car makes less downforce. The result: less grip in corners, more sliding and tyres that overheat.

## Slipstream versus dirty air

On the straights it's the opposite: behind another car there's less drag, so the follower gains speed. That's the slipstream. Overtakes are usually set up in a corner and completed on a straight.

## How to spot it in our charts

In [race pace](/en/analysis), a driver stuck behind another usually sets slower, similar lap times to the car ahead, then pulls away as soon as they're in clean air.
